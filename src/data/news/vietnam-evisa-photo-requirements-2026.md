One bad portrait is among the fastest ways to **delay a Vietnam eVisa**. The online e-visa filing validates uploads electronically; blurry selfies and cropped passport booklet photos often trigger resubmissions or rejection. Get your photo right once—then [start your application](/apply).

**We are a private visa assistance service—not the Government of Vietnam.** Photo rules are set by Vietnamese immigration's eVisa portal; we help you format files correctly before submission.

## What your eVisa portrait should look like

Think **passport studio quality**, not social media:

- **Plain background** — white or off-white; no patterns, furniture, or outdoor scenery.
- **Full face forward** — eyes open, mouth closed, neutral expression.
- **Even lighting** — no harsh shadow on one cheek; no flash reflection on skin.
- **No filters** — beauty modes change skin tone and fail face-matching checks.
- **Recent likeness** — taken within the last six months if your appearance changed (haircut, beard, weight).

## Size, ratio, and file format

Portal limits can change; success usually means:

| Tip            | Detail                                                                                           |
| :------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Dimensions** | Portrait orientation; often ~**600×800 px** or similar—use the exact number on the upload screen |
| **Format**     | **JPEG** unless the portal specifies PNG                                                         |
| **File size**  | Under the stated MB cap; compress without softening eyes or edges                                |
| **Crop**       | Top of hair to chin, slight shoulder visible—face not tiny in frame                              |

If the system shows required pixels, match them exactly—do not guess from old blog screenshots.

## Glasses, head coverings, and children

**Glasses:** Remove non-prescription frames. For prescription lenses, reduce glare or photograph without glasses if the portal allows.

**Head coverings:** Permitted for religious reasons when the **full face** remains visible from hairline to chin.

**Children:** Every child needs their own photo—never a shared selfie with a parent. Baby alone in frame; no toys blocking the face. Parents may support infants from behind a plain sheet—see [Vietnam eVisa for minors and families](/blog/vietnam-evisa-for-minors-families-2026).

## Passport scan vs portrait photo (two different uploads)

| Upload            | What to submit                                                       |
| :---------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bio-page scan** | Full colour passport photo page—MRZ lines, holograms, all corners    |
| **Portrait file** | Separate headshot—not the tiny photo **printed inside** the passport |

Do not swap these files—it is a frequent cause of rejection. Airlines and immigration compare your live face to both images.

For a clean bio-page scan:

- Lay the passport flat on a dark surface and shoot straight-on, not at an angle
- Keep all four corners visible, with no fingers over the data
- Leave a margin below the MRZ (the two lines of chevrons)—tight crops cut it off
- Use colour, not a black-and-white filter

A blurry MRZ can delay approval even when the portrait is perfect.

## Fixing common rejection messages

| Problem                        | Fix                                                                 |
| :----------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Background not uniform         | Hang a white sheet; stand ~1 m from it                              |
| Face too small                 | Crop tighter while keeping full head and shoulders                  |
| File too large                 | Resize to required pixels; JPEG ~85% quality                        |
| Mismatch with passport         | Retake after major appearance change                                |
| Blurry or dark                 | Daylight near a window; hold phone steady                           |
| Glasses glare                  | Retake without glasses, or tilt the frames to remove the reflection |
| Beauty filter or heavy editing | Turn off phone filters and portrait modes; keep natural skin tone   |
| Portrait and scan swapped      | Re-upload each file in its own slot                                 |

## DIY photo setup in five minutes

1. Stand facing a **plain white wall** or sheet.
2. Ask someone to shoot at **eye level**—not from below.
3. Keep **arms down**; face fills roughly 70% of the frame.
4. Export once; zoom to confirm eyes and ears are sharp.
5. Upload on stable Wi‑Fi; re-check passport spelling on the form.

## Photo or passport scan rejected: what to do next

Start with the exact wording of the rejection email or portal message. A portrait problem, an unreadable passport scan, a file-size error, and a face mismatch each need a different fix from the table above.

### Immigration sent a resubmission request

- Fix only the issue named in the message
- Upload before the stated deadline—processing may pause until you respond
- Keep the same reference number; do not open a new application

### Full rejection with no correction path

- Prepare the new portrait and passport scan before paying again
- Re-check name and passport number against the MRZ
- If your flight is soon, choose **Urgent (3 days)** or **Super Urgent (1 day)** when your arrival date allows—see [/fees](/fees)
- [Contact support](/contact) before paying twice

## Frequently asked questions

**What size photo do I need?**  
Use the portal’s stated pixels—typically a tall portrait JPEG under the file-size limit.

**Can I wear glasses?**  
Only if clear and glare-free; many travelers succeed without glasses.

**Can I use my passport’s printed photo?**  
No for the portrait slot—submit a fresh digital headshot.

**Why was my photo rejected?**  
Background, size, filters, glasses glare, mismatch with your passport, or portrait and scan files swapped—retake with plain backdrop and even light.

**Do I need a new application if my photo is rejected?**  
Only after a full rejection. If immigration asks for corrections, upload new files under the same reference number.

**How fast can I fix a rejected photo?**  
Usually the same day—retake, resize, and resubmit. If you must reapply close to departure, check whether Urgent or Super Urgent fits your arrival date.
