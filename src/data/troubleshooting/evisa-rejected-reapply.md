## Top rejection causes (and fixes)

Review generally combines automated checks—file format, image clarity, MRZ readability, basic field validation—with manual review, and security or eligibility databases can flag an application however clean the uploads look. That is why refusal emails are often generic: your job is to work out which category below fits your case.

### Non-compliant portrait photo

**Symptoms:** Rejection without clear text; prior photo resubmission request ignored.

**Fix:**

- White or off-white background, no shadows
- Face forward, eyes open, neutral expression
- No selfies, group crops, glasses glare, non-religious hats, or beauty filters
- Recent photo—not a cropped passport scan

Deep dive: [Vietnam eVisa photo requirements](/blog/vietnam-evisa-photo-requirements-2026).

### Passport validity under six months

**Symptoms:** Rejection near departure; passport expires within six months of arrival.

**Fix:** Renew passport first, then apply with the **new passport number**. Old approvals do not transfer.

Guide: [passport under 6 months](/troubleshooting/expired-passport-error).

### Blurry or cropped passport scan

**Symptoms:** Rejection after manual review; MRZ lines unreadable.

**Fix:**

- Lay the passport flat and capture the full bio page—all four corners and both MRZ lines at the bottom
- Daylight, no flash: glare and shadows break automated text reading
- No fingers or shadows over the passport number
- Export a sharp JPEG within portal size limits—do not over-compress, upscale, or push brightness until text blurs
- Zoom to full size and confirm every MRZ character is legible before upload

### Name, DOB, passport number, or nationality mismatch

**Symptoms:** Quick rejection; data does not match MRZ.

| Field           | Common error                                     | Fix                                     |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| Passport number | Dropped leading zeros; letter O vs zero          | Read the MRZ character by character     |
| Full name       | Missing middle name; wrong order                 | Copy exactly from the bio page          |
| Date of birth   | MM/DD/YYYY habit instead of DD/MM/YYYY           | Use the passport's printed format       |
| Nationality     | Country of residence instead of passport country | Select the nationality on your passport |
| Sex             | M/F does not match passport                      | Match the bio page code                 |

A typo can also slip through to approval, which is worse: airlines match the eVisa to the passport number at check-in, so a mismatched approval can still mean denied boarding. Name and date-of-birth fixes: [wrong name guide](/troubleshooting/wrong-name-evisa).

### Wrong entry port, entry type, or purpose

**Symptoms:** Itinerary does not match the selected port, single vs multiple entry, or travel purpose.

**Fix:**

- Select the **first airport or border** you enter in Vietnam
- Choose multiple entry if you exit and re-enter (side trips to Cambodia, Laos, or Thailand; cruise re-entry)—see [multiple entry guide](/blog/vietnam-evisa-multiple-entry-guide-2026)
- Select business, not tourism, for meetings or commercial activity—tourism does not cover local work
- Port errors on approved visas: [wrong port guide](/troubleshooting/wrong-port-of-entry)

### Duplicate or conflicting applications

**Symptoms:** Rejection after paying twice; extended "processing" then denial.

**Fix:** Contact support before a third filing—see [duplicate application](/troubleshooting/duplicate-evisa-application).

### Security or eligibility screening

**Symptoms:** Rejection without document feedback; rare nationalities or travel history flags.

Typical triggers:

- Passport nationality not eligible for the purpose you selected
- Diplomatic or official passports that require embassy processing
- Prior overstay, deportation, or immigration violation on record
- Passport reported lost or stolen—resolve it with your issuing authority before reapplying
- Several card attempts in a short window, especially over a VPN

**Fix:** Contact support with reference number. Some cases need embassy visa instead of eVisa—confirm eligibility on your [country requirement page](/check-requirement).

## Should you reapply immediately?

| Situation                              | Reapply now?                                          |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Clear photo or passport fix identified | **Yes**—after fixing documents                        |
| Duplicate applications filed           | **Wait**—contact support first                        |
| Rejection reason unknown, same files   | **No**—diagnose before paying again                   |
| Passport under 6 months                | **No**—renew passport first                           |
| Flight within 72 hours                 | **Yes**—Super Urgent after fix, with support guidance |

Some portals show a **24–48 hour cooling-off** after rejection. If unsure, message [support](/contact) with your reference number before a second payment.

## How to reapply without wasting another fee

1. **Do not reuse** the rejected portrait if photo was the issue—take a new compliant shot
2. **Open passport** beside the form—type MRZ data character by character
3. **Select correct port** matching your ticket itinerary
4. **Choose urgency** matching travel date—see [/fees](/fees) and [processing times](/blog/vietnam-evisa-processing-time-2026)
5. **Review confirmation screen** before payment—typos after payment are costly
6. **Save new reference number** separately from the rejected filing

## Reading a rejection email when no reason is listed

Many rejection notices contain only a reference number and the word **Rejected**—no explanation. When that happens, work backward through the highest-probability causes:

1. **Portrait photo** — lighting, background, glasses, crop errors ([photo requirements](/blog/vietnam-evisa-photo-requirements-2026))
2. **Passport scan** — blur, glare, missing MRZ lines
3. **Six-month validity** — counted from arrival date ([passport guide](/troubleshooting/expired-passport-error))
4. **MRZ data** — [name, DOB](/troubleshooting/wrong-name-evisa), passport number
5. **Entry port** — mismatch with real itinerary ([port guide](/troubleshooting/wrong-port-of-entry))
6. **Duplicate filings** — two paid references in a short window ([duplicate guide](/troubleshooting/duplicate-evisa-application))

Fix the most likely item **before** paying again—not all six at random.

## Why second applications fail

Vietnam does not publish eVisa rejection rates. What is consistent is that an identical resubmission tends to repeat the same outcome, while a corrected application addresses the cause.

Second applications fail when travelers:

- Reuse the same rejected portrait file
- Reapply within hours on duplicate records without support clearance
- Change data randomly hoping something "sticks"
- Ignore eligibility limits for their nationality

Treat rejection as **diagnostic feedback**, not bad luck.

## Embassy visa vs eVisa after rejection

Most travelers should fix and reapply for an eVisa. An **embassy or consulate visa** may be the better route when:

- You hold a **diplomatic or official passport** or an unusual travel document
- You need a **long-stay or work category** the eVisa does not cover
- You received repeated rejections without document feedback

Ask support before a third eVisa attempt, and compare pathways: [eVisa vs embassy visa 2026](/blog/vietnam-evisa-vs-embassy-visa-2026).

## Document upgrade checklist before reapply

Use this before authorizing a second payment:

### Portrait photo

- [ ] Plain light background, no shadows on face
- [ ] No filters, hats, or heavy glasses glare
- [ ] Face occupies correct proportion of frame per [photo requirements](/blog/vietnam-evisa-photo-requirements-2026)
- [ ] Taken within last 6 months—not cropped from passport bio page

### Passport scan

- [ ] Full bio page including machine-readable zone at bottom
- [ ] No fingers, glare, or cut-off edges
- [ ] File size and format within portal limits
- [ ] **New passport** scanned if you renewed after first rejection

### Form data

- [ ] Name and DOB match MRZ exactly—[name and DOB guide](/troubleshooting/wrong-name-evisa)
- [ ] Nationality is your passport country, not country of residence
- [ ] Entry port matches ticket or cruise confirmation
- [ ] Single vs multiple entry matches full itinerary
- [ ] Purpose (tourism vs business) matches what you will do

### Application hygiene

- [ ] Only **one** active paid reference unless support approved duplicate void
- [ ] Email address typed correctly—no typo inbox
- [ ] Tier matches travel date—[processing times](/blog/vietnam-evisa-processing-time-2026)

## Refund expectations after rejection

Government eVisa fees are generally **non-refundable** once submitted, regardless of outcome. Private service fees follow your package terms—read [/refund-policy](/refund-policy) before assuming a second payment waives the first.

Focus spend on **fixing the denial cause**, not disputing the first fee, unless your bank shows a duplicate charge for an [Unfulfilled](/troubleshooting/evisa-unfulfilled-status) filing that never completed.

## Will a rejection block future applications?

A single document-based rejection usually does **not** permanently block you. Repeated identical filings, fraud flags, or eligibility issues can trigger longer scrutiny. Fix the cause, space out duplicates, and file one clean corrected application.

## Family or group application with one rejection

When one traveler is refused, the whole trip stalls:

- A child's photo failure can hold the group until that passenger's portrait is resubmitted
- Different entry dates or ports across the group's approvals can split the family at check-in or immigration

Reapply for the refused traveler with the same entry date and port as the rest of the group. Filing tips: [eVisa for minors and families](/blog/vietnam-evisa-for-minors-families-2026).

## After approval: final verification before cruise or flight

Even after a successful **second application**, rejections leave travelers nervous. Before you travel:

1. Read every field on the PDF aloud against your passport MRZ
2. Confirm entry port matches itinerary—especially [cruise ports](/troubleshooting/cruise-ship-evisa-entry)
3. Store PDF in email, cloud, and printed copy
4. Do not check in until the document loads correctly in the airline app

An approval can still fail at the border when the facts do not match it:

| Issue                                     | Result                               |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Arrival at a port not listed on the eVisa | Entry refused                        |
| Passport renewed after applying           | eVisa not valid for the new passport |
| Passport validity under six months        | Boarding or entry denied             |
| Single entry already used, then re-entry  | Re-entry denied                      |
| Work on a tourist eVisa                   | Questioning or refusal               |

A clean second approval is only useful if you present the **matching passport** at every checkpoint.

## Common rejection myths (do not rely on these)

| Myth                             | Reality                                                                 |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "I can explain at the airport"   | Airlines use automated eVisa matching—explanations rarely override data |
| "Rejection means I am banned"    | Most denials are document-level, not permanent bans                     |
| "Third application always works" | Identical errors reject again—fix root cause                            |
| "Travel agent can override"      | Agents cannot change immigration system records at check-in             |
| "Shorter stay lowers scrutiny"   | Stay length rarely fixes photo or passport validity denials             |

When in doubt after a rejection, spend thirty minutes upgrading documents—not thirty seconds re-clicking the pay button.
