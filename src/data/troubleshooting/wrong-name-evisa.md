You noticed a **mistake on your Vietnam eVisa**—a missing middle name, reversed given and family names, a day and month swapped in your date of birth, or a typo you only caught days before your flight. These are among the highest-anxiety visa problems travelers face, and for good reason: airlines deny boarding when eVisa records do not match the passport in their system.

**We are a private visa assistance service—not the Government of Vietnam.** Vietnamese immigration authorities issue and correct eVisas. We help you assess the error, choose the fastest fix, and re-file when a new application is required.

## Quick answer: name and date of birth mistakes

| Situation                                                | Risk level                        | Usual fix                                             |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Typo in surname or given name vs passport                | **High** — boarding denial likely | Correction before approval, or new application        |
| Middle name missing but on passport MRZ                  | **High**                          | Same as above—do not assume it is optional            |
| Accent or hyphen difference (José vs Jose)               | **Medium–High**                   | Match passport print exactly in any re-filing         |
| Name order reversed (family/given swapped)               | **High**                          | Re-file with names in passport order                  |
| Day and month swapped in date of birth (03/07 vs 07/03)  | **High**                          | Correction before approval, or new application        |
| Wrong birth year                                         | **High**                          | New application—rarely corrected after approval       |
| Approved eVisa matches passport; old booking email wrong | **Low**                           | Use the correct PDF; update airline profile if needed |

**Golden rule:** The name and date of birth on your eVisa approval PDF must match the passport you carry at check-in—not an old passport, not a frequent-flyer profile, not your payment receipt.

## Why name and date of birth errors cause denied boarding

International carriers flying to Vietnam run departure control checks against immigration data. Your eVisa links your **passport number**, **name**, and **date of birth** into one identity record. When any of them disagree with your passport—even by one letter or one day—check-in staff often cannot override the mismatch.

Failures happen at three points:

1. **During application** — automated rejection or request to fix data
2. **At airline check-in** — boarding pass withheld
3. **At Vietnam immigration** — entry refused even if the airline let you fly

The worst case is approval with wrong details: you paid, you received an email, but the document is not valid for travel. There is no "close enough" for dates either—**03 July** and **07 March** are different records, so do not count on an officer accepting a one-day difference.

## Common name errors travelers make

### Missing middle name

Many passports—especially from India, the United States, and the UK—print a middle name in the visual zone and MRZ. If your eVisa shows only first and last name, immigration systems may flag a mismatch.

**Check:** Does your MRZ line include more than two name segments? If yes, your eVisa should reflect the same structure.

### Reversed family and given names

Some travelers type given name into the surname field (or vice versa) because everyday usage differs from passport order. Always follow **passport print order**, not how your ticket or hotel booking lists your name.

### Accent marks and special characters

Type names exactly as shown on the passport bio page. If the passport uses plain ASCII (no accents), do not add accents on the form. If accents are printed, include them consistently.

### Maiden name vs married name

Apply using the passport you will **physically present** at the border. If you recently renewed or changed name, use the new booklet—not an old approval tied to a cancelled passport.

## Date of birth errors: the DD/MM/YYYY trap

Many Vietnam eVisa forms ask for dates in **day/month/year** order—the order printed on UK, EU, Australian, and many Asian passports. Travelers used to **month/day/year**, common in the US, often type it backwards:

| Passport shows | Traveler types (US habit) | Result                                                           |
| -------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 07 MAR 1990    | 03/07/1990                | Read as 3 July 1990—accepted by the form, mismatched at check-in |
| 15 AUG 1990    | 08/15/1990                | Usually rejected as invalid—there is no month 15                 |

When the day is 13 or higher, the form usually catches the swap. When it is 12 or lower, the error slips through silently—which is why it often surfaces only at online check-in.

Other common sources of a wrong date of birth:

- **Text months** — if your passport prints "15 AUG 1990", convert it carefully to the form's numeric format
- **Autofill and other documents** — browsers, US-based travel sites, and driver's licences use different formats; type from the passport bio page instead
- **Mobile filing** — swapped digits in the month or year are easy to miss on a small screen; re-read before payment

## What to do right now (step by step)

### If your application is still processing

1. Contact support **immediately** with your reference number
2. Attach a sharp scan of your passport bio page (MRZ visible)
3. List the exact fields that are wrong and the correct details from the passport
4. Do **not** open a second paid application without guidance—duplicates can trigger fraud screening (see the [duplicate application guide](/troubleshooting/duplicate-evisa-application))

Corrections before final approval are sometimes possible when caught early.

### If you already received an approval PDF

1. Compare every name field and the date of birth on the **PDF attachment**—not the portal summary or payment receipt—to your passport
2. If any field differs, **do not fly** with that PDF as your only authorization
3. Contact support about correction options or a rush re-application
4. Select [Super Urgent (1 day)](/apply) or **Urgent (3 days)** if your departure date allows

Serious name or date of birth errors after approval usually require a **new application and new government fee**. Budget for a full re-filing (see [/fees](/fees)), not a quick fix at the airport.

### If your flight is within 48 hours

- Email support with reference number, passport scan, and proof of itinerary
- Apply for the fastest tier your arrival date unlocks on [/apply](/apply)
- Have a backup plan (reschedule, alternate routing) if approval does not arrive before check-in closes

If online check-in flags a "document mismatch," it is not a glitch: the eVisa data does not equal your passport data, and airport counters rarely override it. See also [last-minute Vietnam eVisa options](/faq/24-hour-vietnam-evisa).

## Passport number and multiple-field errors

A wrong **passport number** is equally serious—often worse because the eVisa is electronically keyed to that number. If you renewed your passport, an approval tied to the old booklet needs a new application even when name and date of birth match.

Two or more mismatched fields (for example name and date of birth, or name and passport number) almost always mean a full re-application and can draw extra screening. If only one field is wrong, still contact support before travel—do not self-assess risk at the airport.

## How to prevent name and date of birth mistakes

- Read names from the **MRZ** (bottom two lines of the bio page), not from memory
- Type your date of birth from the passport bio page, in the order the form label asks for (DD/MM/YYYY vs MM/DD/YYYY)
- Turn off browser autofill for name and date fields
- Do not copy from old airline profiles or OTA bookings
- For children, copy name and date of birth from the child's own passport—not from memory (see [eVisa for minors and families](/blog/vietnam-evisa-for-minors-families-2026))
- Review the confirmation screen before payment—corrections after payment are limited
- After approval, put the PDF next to your passport and check name, date of birth (all four digits of the year), and passport number before booking non-refundable hotels
